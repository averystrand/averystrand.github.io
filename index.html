Live products

Three public tools, shipped and running.

Each one is a real application on its own domain, built to earn a prospect's contact information by being useful first. Click any of them. They are all in production.

utilitybillscan.com ↗

Utility bill analyzer

Upload a commercial utility bill, get a findings report. Parses rate schedules, riders, power factor penalties, taxes, demand and consumption charges, and inactive meters straight out of the PDF.

  • Seven-category findings engine with financial impact estimates
  • Claude vision fallback for scanned and photographed bills
  • Token-gated persistent report URLs, rate limited per IP and per domain
  • Lead capture into Salesforce with the report link written onto the Contact
sb253list.com ↗

California SB 253 applicability checker

Look up a company against a disclosure dataset I assembled and find out whether California's climate disclosure law applies to it, plus what it has and has not yet disclosed.

  • Disclosure-gap export for the accounts that are in scope and silent
  • Written vendor-agnostic, so it holds up as a reference rather than a pitch
  • Hedged regulatory language throughout
  • Updated the same day the state moved the reporting deadline
facilityrebates.com ↗

Facility rebate finder

Enter a facility address, get the utility and state efficiency rebates that apply to it. The shortest path from a question a facilities lead actually has to a named, contactable account.

  • Address to utility territory to applicable program matching
  • Same lead capture and alerting stack as the bill analyzer
  • Per-lead identification in PostHog on every report page

Shared infrastructure across all three: lead capture into Salesforce with report links stamped on the Contact, PostHog per-lead identification, Slack alerting, DNS provisioning by API, token-gated report URLs, rate limiting, and nginx and systemd deployment on Linux VMs I administer.

First-party attribution, click to opportunity

Instrumentation

Attribution that died at the landing page, rebuilt end to end. Eight parameters, UTMs plus Google, LinkedIn, and Microsoft click IDs, captured in a 90-day first-party cookie across four web properties and written to dedicated Salesforce fields on every form submission, with flows stamping LeadSource under an overwrite policy I negotiated with marketing.

Closed the conversion feedback loop on three ad platforms: daily server-side Salesforce conversion uploads to Google via the Data Manager API, to OpenAI via its Ads API, and native LinkedIn lead-form sync with an Apex converter that turns a form fill into an attributed Contact in about a minute. Manual CSV reporting replaced with a scheduled report on live GAQL. HubSpot retired from the loop entirely.

The audit that preceded it traced 689 form leads from a $42.7k search campaign to zero opportunities and surfaced $105k of LinkedIn spend hidden behind a default export filter.

Paid media rebuild

Operations as code

Took over a roughly $30k per month program, about $427k of lifetime spend across Google, LinkedIn, and OpenAI ads, from a departing contractor. It had no working conversion tracking and spend figures that changed depending on the source. I rebuilt it in ten weeks into an API-driven, CRM-attributed system.

Stood up Google Ads API access and a scripted operations layer where every change is reviewed in a document, applied by API, and reversible from saved state: cleared stale tracking templates from 18 campaigns, swept 4,560 search terms and applied 46 negative keywords across 9 campaigns after finding 55% of two weeks of search spend going to junk intent, and repointed 18 ads to 16 migrated landing pages behind a URL verification gate with zero failures. Uploaded 2,464 hashed customer records as a Customer Match list and attached it as an exclusion on every live campaign.

The point of the rebuild is that kill-or-scale decisions now rest on CRM-verified conversions instead of platform-reported soft events. Measurement is live; the attributed-revenue verdict is still being earned.

An agent-operated codebase

How I work

I built the GTM monorepo to be operated by an AI agent with a human directing and approving. 34 task-specific skills, a structured knowledge vault covering ICP, personas, competitive intelligence, and founder voice guides mined from call transcripts, and guardrails implemented as code rather than policy: no outbound send and no ad-account mutation without a review document and explicit approval, every CRM change written as a changeset first, before and after exports on every mutation.

The Anthropic SDK is embedded in the shipped products for document parsing, classification, and summarization. The same thinking changed how the team buys software: tool purchasing is now API and MCP first, so anything we pay for stays addressable. The guardrails matter more than the speed. Anything that touches a customer record or spends money is gated on a human reading a document first.
