Separately, the closed-lost re-approach family I wrote for the BDR team (10 sequences, 711 contacts, 1,960 sends) runs at 56.4% open and 5.3% reply. The Climate Week cold sequences booked four meetings that became $200k of Meeting Booked pipeline.

Two paired A/B tests

Each cold play ships as a pair. The question in both of these was whether a personalized gift-card offer (a named local donut shop, pulled per contact from the CRM) beats a generic one. It did not. The generic variant won on reply rate both times, so it became the default and the enrichment step was dropped.

How it runs

Audiences come out of Clay, Unify, Salesforce, and public datasets (event attendee lists, follower scrapes, competitor customer bases, utility commission dockets), scored on title and firmographics against the ICP, then suppressed against customers, open opportunities, competitors, and recent touches in code before anyone is enrolled. Copy is written as a sequence, with per-person lines generated by an agent I direct and delivered through the Unify API into standard fields, so the sequence body is pure merge fields. Nothing sends without a review document and an explicit go. Replies and meetings are stamped to the opportunity in Salesforce so each play reports its own pipeline.

The LinkedIn side runs on the same control panel: new-follower enrichment, post-engagement diffing that turns likes and comments into dated intent events, industry nurture tracks (connect, DM, bump) through PhantomBuster, and a competitor seller-network watch. More on all of that on the main page.
